Invention and innovation in Schizocosa floridana

Innovation on a theme can push signals into new areas, but new signals built on the infrastructure of the old are likely to inherit the same general shape, the same constraints.

By inventing a whole new way to make sound, or a whole new ornament, on the other hand, animals can take a leap into completely new phenotypic space.

Listen to my favorite spider, Schizocosa floridana:

VIDEO OR AUDIO: the song

Their song has its particular versions of the same thumping, tapping sounds that are common in the genus. They’re unique to the species, but recognizable in their rough, broadband way.

But it also has something really different: the chirp.

VIDEO: the chirp

That’s a new sound. None of S. floridana’s close relatives make it. It’s pure tone, like a musical note. It’s not just a new sound, it’s a new way of making sound. So new, in fact, that I have no idea how it works.

We’ve eliminated the usual suspects. But at the moment, we only know what it’s not, not what it is. But whatever the mechanism, this new sound has unlocked a new axis of variation: tonality. Unlike its relatives, S. floridana song varies not only in timing and loudness, but in pitch.

There are lots of questions here without answers, which is part of the fun. But one thing that I wonder about is, why floridana? Why does one species evolve something new, where other related species do not?

That’s another question without an answer, but I do have the beginning of one. Or, perhaps just the beginning of more questions.
